# B2B Sales Playbook

## Pipeline stages

1. **Prospecting** — lead received, first contact attempted.
2. **Qualification** — need, budget, authority and timeline confirmed.
3. **Proposal** — site survey done, quotation sent.
4. **Negotiation** — customer is discussing price, scope or terms.
5. **Closed won / Closed lost**.

## Response-time standards

- Respond to every new inbound lead within 4 business hours.
- Reply to any customer question or request within 24 business hours. Customers waiting more than a
  day for an answer are the single biggest predictor of a lost deal in our history.
- Follow up on every sent quotation within 3 business days if the customer has not responded.
- Never let a negotiation-stage deal go 5 days without a customer touchpoint.

## Follow-up cadence after a quotation

| Day after quote | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Confirm receipt; offer a walkthrough call |
| Day 3 | Call the decision maker; ask about open questions |
| Day 7 | Share a relevant case study and ask for a decision timeline |
| Day 14 | Manager-to-manager call for deals above ₹3,00,000 |

## Deals at risk

A deal is at risk when any of the following is true: the customer is waiting for a reply, a quote has
gone unanswered for more than 5 days, a competitor has been mentioned, the customer raised a price
objection, or the follow-up task is overdue. At-risk deals above ₹3,00,000 must be reviewed by the
Sales Manager within one business day.

## Escalation

Escalate to the Sales Manager when the customer requests a discount above 10%, mentions a competing
quotation, asks for non-standard payment terms, or when a high-value deal (₹3,00,000 or more) has had
no contact for 7 days.

## Buying signals

Strong buying signals include: asking for PO format or payment details, asking for installation dates,
mentioning an internal deadline (audit, board meeting, quarter end), involving procurement, and asking
for references. A customer showing buying signals who has not heard back from us is the highest
priority in the pipeline.
