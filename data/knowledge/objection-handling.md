# Objection Handling Guide

## Price objection ("your price is too high")

1. Acknowledge the concern without arguing.
2. Ask which line items drive the concern and what the approved budget is.
3. Re-establish value: typical Arcline customers reduce energy cost by 8–12% in the first year and
   avoid unplanned downtime through vibration alerts. Use the customer's own numbers where possible.
4. Offer scope options before price: phase the rollout (start with fewer sites), move from
   Enterprise to Standard licence, or remove optional training.
5. Only if needed, request a discount within the Discount Authority Policy. Never promise a discount
   above 10% before manager approval.

## Competitor mentioned / lower competing quotation

1. Thank the customer for being transparent; never disparage a competitor.
2. Ask for the competing quotation or its scope, and compare line by line. Competing offers frequently
   exclude commissioning, operator training, alerting, or multi-site dashboards.
3. Highlight differentiators the customer has already said they value (for example, the dashboard
   or alerting).
4. Offer a reference call with a customer in the same industry.
5. If a price gap remains after scope alignment, involve the Sales Manager; a discount above 10%
   requires manager approval.
6. Respond the same day — a customer who mentions a competitor and receives no reply is very likely to
   choose the competitor.

## Timing / budget objection ("not this quarter")

Propose a phased rollout or a pilot on one site, and agree on a specific date to revisit. Create a
follow-up task for that date.

## Authority objection ("I need to check with management")

Offer to present to the decision maker directly, and send a one-page business case the contact can
forward internally.

## Clarification requests

Questions about scope, payment terms or licensing are buying signals. Answer them within 24 business
hours with specifics from the Pricing & Quotation Policy.
